Move-In Decorating Notes

Decorating a house after you move in: what to do first

The first weeks in a new house are when the paint colours bother you most. The previous owner's choices are on every wall, and it is tempting to start with a roller on the first free weekend. A little patience at the start usually gives a better result and saves painting the same room twice.

Live with it for a few weeks

Rooms look different at different times of day. A colour that seems right on a sample card can look flat in a north-facing room or too strong in a sunny one. Living in the house for a few weeks shows where the light falls, which rooms get used most and where the furniture will actually go.

Fix things before you paint

Paint shows up every flaw underneath it. Look for cracks in ceilings and around door frames, blown plaster that sounds hollow when tapped, and stains that could point to an old leak. Damp patches need their cause found and fixed first, or the stain will come back through the new paint. Any electrical work, new sockets or plumbing changes are best done now too, because they usually mean cutting into walls.

Work from the top down

Ceilings come first, then walls, then woodwork such as skirting boards, doors and frames. Drips and splashes fall downwards, so this order means mistakes land on surfaces that have not been finished yet. New flooring is normally laid after the decorating, so it does not get paint on it.

Which rooms first

Bedrooms are often a good place to start, because a calm, finished room to sleep in makes the rest of the work easier to live with. Hallways and stairs take the most knocks while furniture is moved in and out, so many people leave them until last.

Preparation takes the most time

Filling, sanding, washing down walls and masking edges takes longer than the painting itself. Fresh plaster needs to dry fully and usually needs a mist coat before the main colour goes on. Gloss or satin on old woodwork needs a light sand so the new coat grips.
